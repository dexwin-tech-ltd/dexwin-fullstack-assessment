What you would cover before release overnight:

Basic functionalities. I would navigate through the critical and happy paths that show the main functionality of the app. Are end users able to navigate between the project available? Are they able to see the tasks under each project and their details. Are they able to identify who's working on the task, the current status of the tasks, the task priority and any comments that the task owner may have. Are they able to update the status of tasks once completed? Or to reopen tasks that may not have been completed properly.

What you would skip and why

If time was a burden, I would skip all the cosmetic issues, including making the UI more appealing. 

Highest risks if we ship as-is

Users are not able to identify the different tasks available, and what is required of them. No details as to work expected is provided. There may be clashes in terms of what users are working on and their priorities since details are left out. Timelines may not be met, as the tasks don't show an accurate status on them, to help project managers determine if the team will meet timelines or deadlines. Unauthorised users being able to access sensitive project details

Suggested go / no-go

Fix critical paths. Allow users to do basic functionalities. And then push to production before fixing other minor issues. Do not release until basic functionalities are working.
